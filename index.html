What this is. A feed on the advertising industries worldwide, in twenty subjects, following the arc the section it comes from traces. It begins with opinion engineered on purpose — the Creel Committee, Bernays, the discovery during the First World War that mass desire could be manufactured — and the psychological technique that followed. Then marketing aimed at children, tobacco and alcohol marketing and the fake research and manufactured doubt that protected it. Then the same methods optimised: behavioural tracking, microtargeting aimed at whoever is most exploitable, feeds built for engagement, and the data supply chain underneath all of it. Then who owns the agencies, the platforms that sell the space and their search share, the shaping of what surfaces, green claims that do not hold, advertising that does not look like advertising, political advertising, AI both as a tool and as something being steered, advertising in shared space, and advertising beyond Earth. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

Advertising in shared space and beyond it are subjects, not curiosities. The section's objection is not only to what advertising says but to where it is put — that placing a lure inside someone else's field of view is itself the act, since there is no way to know what is there before you look. So advertising in shared space follows billboards, transit, naming rights and the cities restricting them, and advertising beyond Earth follows orbital billboards and satellite brightness, along with the companies the section names as working on them. Both are small subjects by volume and central to the argument.

What it is not. The trade press is not this subject, and it is most advertising coverage: campaign of the week, account wins, creative hires, Cannes Lions, best-ads round-ups, rebrands, marketing tips. All refused, along with ordinary business coverage carrying no conduct angle. What this wire keeps is how attention is taken, from whom, by what technique, and under what rules. A story no subject will claim is refused rather than filed under a name it did not earn.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Regulators, courts & data authorities is the bodies that rule on it — advertising standards regulators, data protection authorities, competition authorities and courts. Research & influence tracking is the tracking and adtech research, and the work on industry concentration. Privacy & consumer organisations is those bringing the complaints. Press is general news across 26 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a fine, an ad banned, a complaint upheld, a law enacted, a practice prohibited (2); institutional material — a regulator or data authority finding, an OECD or audit-office report, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure such as ad spend, market share or exposure (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential.

What is refused. The trade press, awards and campaign round-ups, marketing how-tos, business coverage with no conduct angle, sponsored content and market copy. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.

Harvested every two hours from advertising and data protection regulators, competition authorities and courts, tracking and adtech research, privacy and consumer organisations, regional editions in 26 languages, and dedicated subject searches. Headlines belong to their publishers.
