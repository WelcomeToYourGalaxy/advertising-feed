# advertising-feed

A live wire on the advertising industries worldwide: how attention is taken, by
whom, using what technique, and under what rules.

Built after "The Advertising Industries" on Welcome to Your Galaxy, following
the arc that section traces — from opinion engineered on purpose during the
First World War, through the psychological technique that followed and its
export worldwide, to the same methods optimised by behavioural tracking and
algorithmic feeds.

## The twenty subjects

| | |
|---|---|
| Engineering opinion and desire | Psychological technique as method |
| Marketing aimed at children | Tobacco, alcohol, gambling and the rest |
| Funded research and manufactured doubt | Behavioural tracking |
| Microtargeting and who gets picked out | Attention, addiction and the feed |
| The data supply chain | Who owns the industry |
| The platforms that sell the space | Shaping what surfaces |
| Claims that do not hold | Advertising that does not look like it |
| Political advertising | Automation, generation and steering |
| Advertising in shared space | Advertising beyond Earth |
| Rules, bans and enforcement | What is set against it |

Advertising in shared space and beyond it are carried as subjects rather than
curiosities. The section's objection is not only to what advertising says but
to where it is put — placing a lure inside someone else's field of view is
itself the act, since there is no way to know what is there before you look.
So one subject follows billboards, transit, naming rights and the cities
restricting them, and another follows orbital billboards and satellite
brightness, including the companies the section names as working on them.

## The gate

The trade press is refused, and it is most advertising coverage: campaign of
the week, account wins, creative hires, Cannes Lions, best-ads round-ups,
rebrands, marketing how-tos. So is business coverage with no conduct angle.

A story no subject will claim is refused and counted as refused, rather than
filed under a fallback subject it did not earn.

## Weight

A decision (2), institutional material (2), a measured figure (1), a pending
decision with a date (1), a named jurisdiction (1), a primary source (1). At
three or more it is marked consequential.
